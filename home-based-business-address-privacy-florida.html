Home-Based Florida Businesses and Address Privacy

Running your company from home? Here is which Florida filing fields go public, and how a registered agent helps keep your residence out of view.

Thousands of Florida businesses start at a kitchen table. Consultants, online sellers, contractors, designers and bookkeepers often run their companies from home for years. It is a sensible way to keep costs down, but it raises a question many owners only think about after forming their LLC: whose address ends up on the public record, and who can see it? This page explains how Florida filings expose addresses and what a registered agent can and cannot do about it.

Why Florida business records are so visible

Florida runs one of the most open business registries in the country. Anyone can search Sunbiz, the Division of Corporations database, free of charge and without an account. A typical LLC record displays the entity name, filing history, principal office address, mailing address, registered agent name and address, and the names and addresses of authorized persons or managers.

That openness serves a purpose: it lets courts, creditors and customers find out who stands behind a business. But it also means that data brokers, marketers and anyone curious can collect those addresses in bulk. For a home-based business, every address field you fill in with your residence is one more public pointer to where you live.

What the registered agent address covers

The registered office must be a physical street address in Florida where the agent is available during business hours. If you serve as your own agent from home, your house becomes that address, and process servers will arrive at your front door. If you hire a commercial agent, the agent's office address appears instead.

That substitution keeps your home off one prominent line of the record and keeps legal deliveries away from your family and neighbors. It also means you do not need to be home all day to meet the availability requirement.

Fields a registered agent does not replace

A registered agent is not a complete privacy solution, and it is worth being clear about the limits:

  • Principal office address. Florida asks for the street address of your principal place of business. If you truly operate from home, that is your home. Some agents offer a separate virtual office or business address service for this purpose, usually at extra cost, and you should confirm it is appropriate for your situation.
  • Mailing address. This can often be a separate address, such as a mail service or a P.O. box, since it is simply where the state sends correspondence.
  • Manager and member addresses. Florida filings list addresses for the people named as authorized representatives. Many owners use a business address here rather than a residence where permitted.

Once an address is filed, it tends to live on in historical filings and third-party databases. It is far easier to keep a home address private from the start than to scrub it later. Protecting home address privacy for LLC owners is one of the main reasons people hire a professional agent.

Practical steps for home-based owners

If privacy matters to you, plan your filing before you submit it. Start by hiring a commercial registered agent so legal papers go to a staffed office. Next, decide what you will use for the mailing address and for the addresses of managers or members. Then consider whether a business address service is worthwhile for your principal office, bearing in mind that local licensing, banking and tax registrations may still require your actual location.

Keep your filings consistent. An address that appears on Sunbiz should match what you give your bank and the Department of Revenue, or at least be explainable. Mismatches can slow down account openings and licensing reviews.

Beyond the state filing

Sunbiz is not the only place a home address leaks. Local business tax receipts issued by Florida counties and cities, professional licenses, domain registrations and Google business listings can all display it. If you are serious about separating home and business, review each of these as well. Many owners use one business address consistently across all of them, which also makes the company look more established to customers.

Is the extra cost justified?

For most home-based owners, yes. A commercial registered agent costs less each year than many software subscriptions, and it keeps process servers and a public pointer to your residence away from your household. Pair it with a sensible approach to the remaining address fields and your Florida LLC can stay professional on paper while you keep working from home.
