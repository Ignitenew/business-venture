Registered Agent Costs in Florida, Explained

From free DIY to premium plans, here is what you actually pay for a Florida registered agent and where the extra charges hide.

The cost of a registered agent in Florida is one of those small, recurring expenses that rarely gets much scrutiny. That is a mistake, because the gap between a well-priced plan and a poorly understood one can add up to hundreds of dollars over the life of a business. This page breaks down what you actually pay for, where the hidden charges tend to live, and how the agent fee fits alongside the state's own fees.

The three ways to cover the role

Florida lets any adult resident with a physical Florida street address, or any business authorized to act as an agent in the state, serve as your registered agent. That gives you three basic options, each with a different cost profile.

Acting as your own agent

This is free in dollar terms. You list your own Florida address and accept papers yourself. The real cost is in availability and privacy: you must be reachable at that address during business hours, and the address becomes public on Sunbiz.

Naming a friend, relative or your attorney

Often free or bundled into a legal retainer. It works while the arrangement holds, but people move, change jobs and forget to pass along mail. Some law firms charge a modest annual fee for the service.

Hiring a commercial registered agent

Commercial agents typically charge somewhere in the range of fifty to three hundred dollars per year in Florida. In exchange you get a staffed office, online document access and, usually, compliance reminders.

What drives the price of a commercial agent

Low-cost agents and premium agents perform the same legal function. The price difference comes from what surrounds that function. The most common factors include:

  • Scanning scope. Some plans scan only service of process; others scan every piece of mail.
  • Mail forwarding. Physical forwarding of originals is often billed per shipment plus postage.
  • Compliance tools. Annual report reminders, filing assistance and document templates may be included or sold separately.
  • Support. Access to knowledgeable staff by phone tends to cost more than email-only help.
  • Multi-state discounts. If you are registered in several states, bundles can lower the per-state cost.

Watch for introductory pricing

Free first-year offers are common, particularly when an agent is bundled with LLC formation. There is nothing wrong with taking one, as long as you know the renewal rate. Many of these plans renew automatically at full price, and the renewal can land on a card you have since replaced. Write down the renewal amount and date the day you sign up. Understanding registered agent pricing in Florida is only one part of choosing the right provider.

Also check whether the price is guaranteed. A few providers lock the annual fee for as long as you stay with them, while others reserve the right to raise it. Over five or ten years, that difference matters more than a one-time discount.

State fees that sit alongside the agent fee

The agent fee is separate from what you pay the State of Florida. For an LLC, the main recurring state cost is the annual report, which runs $138.75 and must be filed between January 1 and May 1. File late and Florida adds a $400 late fee. Corporations pay a different annual report fee, and nonprofits pay less.

If you decide to change registered agents after formation, you will file a statement of change with the Division of Corporations, which carries its own small filing fee. Some new agents will prepare and submit this paperwork for you at no extra charge, which is worth asking about.

Calculating the true cost

A simple way to compare plans is to estimate a three-year total. Add the first-year price, two years of renewals, and a realistic estimate of add-ons such as forwarded envelopes or extra scans. A plan advertised at a slightly higher price but including unlimited scanning often beats a cheaper plan once you account for actual mail volume.

Tip: ask each provider what a typical customer with your type of business pays per year in total. A provider confident in its pricing will give a straight answer.

Is a paid agent worth it?

For a business run from a commercial office with regular staffed hours, acting as your own agent can be perfectly workable. For a home-based business, a business owner who travels, or anyone who would rather not have legal papers delivered in front of customers, the annual fee buys reliability and privacy. Viewed against the potential cost of a missed lawsuit or a dissolved entity, most owners find the expense easy to justify.
